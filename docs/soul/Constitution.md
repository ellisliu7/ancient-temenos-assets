# Constitution

Tier: Soul
Changes: almost never
Last reviewed: 2026-06-18
If this changes, it means: the project's identity has fundamentally shifted

---

## What this document is

This is the law of Ancient Temenos. It does not describe what we hope to feel; it
constrains what we are allowed to do — especially the future, more tired, more tempted
version of us who will one day have a good reason to break one of these lines. Every
principle below is written so a violation would be visible. If a principle cannot be
broken, it is not a law. It is decoration, and it does not belong here.

These are the seven principles first drafted for this project, refined and given teeth,
with two additions that were missing: the north star they all serve, and the duty of care
a sanctuary owes anyone who arrives in danger.

---

## The North Star

Everything here serves one person: someone we love, on the worst night of their life, who
has found their way in and needs to be held until morning.

Every decision is tested against them. Not against us.

---

## The Principles

**1. First, we do no harm.**
People will arrive in real distress. The sanctuary never pretends to be a therapist, never
gives medical or crisis guidance, never deepens despair, and never keeps a person in the
room when what they need is a living human being. When someone is in danger, the only task
is to be gentle and to point them, without obstruction, toward real help. A sanctuary that
can wound the person it was built for has stopped being one.

**2. The sanctuary is free to enter.**
Being met, being seen, being returned to yourself — the heart of this place is never placed
behind payment, an account, or any gate but honesty. If someone in need reaches a wall
before they reach the heart, we have failed the one person this was built for.

**3. The system is the sanctuary, and we keep it sound.**
There is no sanctuary above the code, the words, the way it moves. A beautiful room with a
broken door is a broken room. Excellence in how it works is not separate from the soul. The
soul is made of it.

**4. We give before we ask.**
Reflection comes before any request for support — in time and in importance. We ask only
after the value has landed, quietly, framed as helping the place survive for the next person
who needs it. Never as a price. Never as a way to unlock what was already theirs. Never in a
way that breaks the silence.

**5. What a person keeps commemorates; it never permits.**
Anything someone can collect is made from their own honesty, in the moment they show up — a
record of what they did, never a key to what they are owed. Nothing collected here grants
access to anything. The price of a keepsake is presence, not money.

**6. How we earn shapes what we make, so we earn with care.**
Money is a force, not a neutral helper. It bends whatever it touches toward what it rewards.
We accept only money whose incentives point the same way as the soul of this place, and we
refuse any money that would require us to optimize a person instead of serve them —
including capital that demands we grow faster than the work can stay true.

**7. Every feature must earn its place against the silence it replaces.**
Almost anything can be argued to deepen wonder, reflection, or connection. That is not the
test. The test is whether it does more good than the quiet it displaces. What we refuse
defines this place as much as what we build. It whispers. It never shouts.

**8. We protect meaningful friction and remove only obstruction.**
The threshold, the honest question, the slowness, the pause held a beat too long — these are
not obstacles. They are the sacredness, and we guard them. We remove only the friction that
gets in the way without giving meaning. Frictionlessness is not a virtue here. It is the
texture of the things we are refusing to become.

---

## The Test

For any decision: *Would the person this was built for, on their worst night, be served by it?*

Not *would we enjoy it,* and not *would we use it.* We are not the measure of a place built
for someone else, on a night we are not having. The earlier test — "would El use this?" — is
retired, because it quietly made the author the measure of the sanctuary, and the author is
the least likely person to ever arrive here in real need.

---

## What this is not allowed to forget

ARC put access before transformation. The danger here is the opposite mistake: putting
transformation before survival. Both can kill the thing. A sanctuary that gates the medicine
fails the person at the door. A sanctuary too pure to fund itself fails the person who
arrives after it has gone dark.

So the whole of it, in one line:

> Free to enter. Excellent enough to trust. Safe enough for someone in crisis. Funded
> honestly enough to still exist when that person finally needs it.

---

## Amendment

This document changes almost never. It is amended only in daylight, and never under
pressure — not a deadline, not a funding gap, not a growth target — because those are the
exact forces it exists to withstand. Any change is dated here and logged in Decisions, with
the reasoning preserved. The principles may be sharpened. They may not be quietly softened.
