# Sustainability

Tier: Canon
Changes: rarely (the principles); implementation lives in the working layer
Last reviewed: 2026-06-18
If this changes, it means: the way the sanctuary keeps existing has been deliberately rethought

---

## What this document assumes

The money lines in the Constitution are fixed and not restated here: the sanctuary is free to
enter, what a person keeps commemorates rather than permits, we give before we ask, and we
refuse money that would require us to optimize a person instead of serve them. This document
is the next layer down — how the place actually keeps existing *within* those lines.

## The governing question

Not "how do we monetize the sanctuary," which is the question inherited from a different kind
of product. The right question is quieter: **what does this need to keep existing?** The
answer is usually far less than a business, and it is allowed to be. A place built to whisper
was probably never meant to scale like one that shouts. Making peace with that is part of
keeping it true.

## Where support may come from

Money flows from gratitude, never from desire for access. The models that fit:

- **Patronage.** People moved by the place sustain it, so it stays free for the next person.
  The gift comes after the experience, never before, and never as a gate.
- **Belonging, not access.** If there is ever a membership, it buys *more of the maker* —
  process, the making of the art, early sight of new rooms, a name kept in the temple — never
  more transformation. No one ever pays to be met more deeply.
- **Keepsakes.** Relics made from a person's own honesty, kept because they want a piece of
  what moved them. They unlock nothing. (See Constitution, Principle 5.)
- **Commissioned and physical work.** The maker is an artist: bespoke pieces, prints, a
  printed grimoire — objects that carry the place into the world.

## How the ask is made

Only after the value has landed. Framed as keeping the sanctuary alive for whoever comes
next, never as a price and never as guilt. With a gentle suggested figure rather than a blank
field, because a blank field is decision-fatigue at the exact wrong moment. Quietly enough
that it never breaks the spell. The ask is itself part of the place, or it does not belong in it.

## What we refuse

Advertising. Engagement, habit, or daily-return optimization. Selling the core experience.
Capital that requires growth faster than the work can stay true. Anything that turns a person
into a metric. (The principle lives in the Constitution; this is its working edge.)

## The honest risks

Patronage is real but thin, especially early. When a price is left fully open, most people
pay nothing — and the model that works for an established, beloved name does **not** transfer
to a place starting from zero. It runs on existing love, which has to be earned first. So
patronage alone is unlikely to be enough at the start. Expect to need more than one stream,
and expect the keepsake and commission layers to carry more of the weight than the donation
layer does.

## The tension we are not pretending away

The gift economy tends to reward the loud, unashamed, repeated ask. Ancient Temenos whispers.
These pull against each other, and it is not yet resolved. We will not solve it by shouting,
and we may not survive by staying silent. The work is to find the third thing — a way to ask
that is honest and clear without becoming a sales voice. Left open on purpose.

## Sequence

Finish the free sanctuary first. Let real people feel it. Design the actual model from the
gratitude you observe, not from mechanics invented in advance. Building the collection plate
before the temple is finished is how the soul drifts, and how energy gets spent on a problem
that doesn't exist yet.

## How we will know it is working

Not by revenue, growth, or how often people return. By a simpler measure: the place is still
here, still true, and still free, on the night someone finally needs it. That is the only
number that matters.
