# ✦ Ancient Temenos — Artist Positioning

*Guides all future planning around pricing, editions, patronage,
collector offerings, artwork sales, and revenue.*

---

## The Core Premise

Ancient Temenos was built around an existing art practice.
The artwork is not decoration for the temple.
The temple is a context for the artwork.

El is a professional exhibiting artist with a collector history.
Original works have already been sold.
This is not a first product launch.
It is an extension of an existing artistic life into a new medium.

All pricing, edition decisions, and collector mechanics must be positioned
relative to fine art, galleries, patronage, and serious collecting —
not creator merchandise, fandom products, or digital drops.

**The primary goal is not conversion volume.
It is artistic integrity, collector trust, and long-term value.**

---

## The Three Offering Tiers

### I. Original Graphite Works

These are singular objects.
There is only one.
That scarcity is not a marketing decision — it is already true.

- **Price range:** $4,000–$9,000 USD
- **Positioning:** Fine art primary market. Comparable to mid-career gallery pricing.
- **Who collects these:** Serious collectors, patrons, people who want the irreducible thing.
- **How they are offered:** Not through a button. Through a conversation, a private page, or a direct inquiry. The acquisition process should carry weight proportional to the work.
- **Provenance:** Each original includes documentation of the chamber it belongs to, the mythology it holds, and El's hand.

Original works are never discounted.
Original works are never bundled.
Original works do not appear in the same UI layer as prints.

---

### II. Limited Edition Prints

The print is not competing with the original.
The print is: *"I want to carry a piece of this chamber home."*
That is a different impulse and a different product.

- **Edition size:** 22 per artwork
  - *Why 22:* Master Builder number in numerology. The number of the architect. Fits the sacred geometry sensibility of the temple without requiring explanation to a collector who knows.
- **Price range:** $333–$444 USD
  - $333 — entry point, Edition of 22, unsigned digital certificate
  - $444 — includes a handwritten note from El, signed, with a short oracle inscription chosen for the collector
  - *The $111 gap between tiers carries meaning. The difference is El's hand.*
- **Positioning:** Art print market, not merchandise. Comparable to small-edition prints from gallery-represented artists.
- **What a print includes:** High-quality archival print (production TBD), edition number, chamber name, year.
- **How they are offered:** Through the Grimoire closing moment — after the oracle session, when the visitor has received something. Not before. Not in a shop.

Prints are never offered as a default or a fallback.
They are offered at the moment of maximum resonance.

---

### III. Chamber Keys (Architecture — Not Yet Built)

Keys are not the thing being collected.
Keys open things.

- The **Ancient Temenos Key** is the root identity layer — membership, access, belonging.
- **Chamber Keys** (Venus Key, Ganymede Key, future chambers) are records of a visit. Proof of having passed through. They may eventually unlock things that only exist inside the temple.
- Keys are *earned or activated through the visit*, not purchased separately from it.
- No burn mechanics. No scarcity drops. No FOMO.
- **Status:** Architectural direction only. No mechanics to build yet. Revisit when real visitor behavior exists.

The key and the artwork are separate systems.
Do not conflate them.

---

## Where Artwork Appears in the Journey

The artwork is already in the temple — on the altars in the foyer, as the visual soul of each chamber.

The collector offering appears at one moment only:
**The Grimoire closing screen.**

After the seeker has received something from the oracle. After they have sealed their session.
At the moment they are looking for something to carry home.

That is when the print is shown.
Not as a purchase button.
As a question:
> *"Would you like to carry a piece of this chamber with you?"*

The original works live on a separate Artist page — linked from the foyer or the about layer, never from inside a session.
They are for people who have already decided.

---

## Pricing Philosophy

Do not price to convert.
Price to select.

The price of a print should be high enough that buying it means something.
The price of an original should reflect that this is El's actual artistic work,
not a digital asset or a side product.

Resist:
- Discounts and promotions
- Bundle deals
- Urgency mechanics ("only 3 left!")
- Edition inflation (starting at 100 to "test demand")

These are not moves available to an artist who values her work.

**Long-term collector value is protected by integrity in the short term.**

---

## What Is Not Being Built

Not building:
- A shop or storefront layer inside the temple
- NFT drop mechanics or wallet burns
- Subscription tiers based on edition ownership
- Collector leaderboards or social proof
- Open editions or print-on-demand

These are all moves that would collapse the positioning from *fine art* to *creator product.*

---

## The Collective Memory Question

Collective Memory (the idea that keys, visits, and artwork might eventually connect across visitors) is a real and interesting direction.

It is not being built yet.

Before building connective infrastructure, the question is:
*Does a single visitor complete a full arc — from foyer to oracle to grimoire to artwork — and find it meaningful enough to collect?*

That is the only experiment that matters now.

---

## Revenue — Guiding Principles

1. **Demand before infrastructure.** Prove a visitor will collect before building the collection system.
2. **El's hand is the premium.** Every tier with her direct involvement commands a higher price.
3. **Scarcity that is already true is more powerful than manufactured scarcity.** There is one original. That is enough.
4. **The session earns the offer.** Nothing is offered before the visitor has received something real.
5. **Earliness cannot be bought back.** The first 22 collectors of any edition hold an irreplaceable position. Name that.
