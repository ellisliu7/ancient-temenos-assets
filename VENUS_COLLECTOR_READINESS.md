## Readiness Checklist

Work through this before the inquiry experiment goes live in the Grimoire.
Every ✗ is a gap that will show in the first collector conversation.

### The Original
- [ ] Exact dimensions measured and written down (height × width, cm and inches)
- [ ] Medium described precisely (e.g. "graphite on 300gsm acid-free cotton rag")
- [ ] Year completed confirmed
- [ ] Physical condition noted honestly (any foxing, handling marks, anything a collector would want to know)
- [ ] Current location (studio? stored? framed?)
- [ ] Framing status (framed / unframed / available framed on request)
- [ ] Exhibition history, if any (venue, year, title of show)
- [ ] Prior sale history, if any (sold and returned to El? never sold? first time offered?)
- [ ] High-resolution photograph taken — see Photography section below
- [ ] Price decided privately and written in this document

### The Print Edition
- [ ] Print dimensions decided (one size only — no variants for this edition)
- [ ] Paper decided (archival cotton rag, minimum 300gsm, acid-free, suitable for giclée)
- [ ] Process confirmed (giclée / archival inkjet)
- [ ] At least two print production partners researched (local fine art printer and one online)
- [ ] Sample ordered from chosen printer (not required before first enquiry, but before first print ships)
- [ ] Signing protocol decided (El signs recto bottom right, edition number bottom left, or certificate only)
- [ ] Affirmation line protocol decided — where it appears (on certificate vs. handwritten note)
- [ ] Certificate template drafted — see Certificate section below
- [ ] Price decided privately and written in this document
- [ ] Shipping method decided (rolled archival tube — standard; not flat-packed at this stage)
- [ ] Shipping cost estimated for key regions (Singapore, UK, US, EU — these are most likely)
- [ ] Payment method decided for print transactions (Stripe link, bank transfer, or PayPal for international)

### Photography (the most urgent gap)
- [ ] High-resolution photograph of the original — full work, even lighting, no glare, neutral background
- [ ] Detail photograph — one close crop showing graphite texture and mark-making
- [ ] Scale photograph (optional but useful) — work photographed with something indicating size
- [ ] All photographs saved as TIFF or maximum-quality JPEG, minimum 3000px on the long edge
- [ ] Photographs named clearly: `Venus_Original_Full.jpg`, `Venus_Original_Detail.jpg`

### Collector Assets Folder
- [ ] Folder created — see structure below
- [ ] All documents drafted or complete
- [ ] Folder backed up (not only on one device)

### Response Readiness
- [ ] Response email drafted — see below
- [ ] El's email address confirmed as the enquiry destination
- [ ] El has committed to responding within 24 hours of the first enquiry

## Decisions — Lock Now vs. Later

### Lock before the inquiry experiment goes live

These will be asked in the first email. Not having answers reads as unpreparedness.

**Price of the print.** Commit to one number. Suggested: $444. Write it down. Do not revisit until the edition closes.

**Price of the original.** You do not need to publish it. You need to know it. Have a number in your head before the first email arrives. Arriving at the price during a collector conversation is the fastest way to lose the collector.

**Edition size.** Already decided: 22. This is now permanent. Say so plainly in every conversation.

**What the print includes.** Decide: signed, numbered, with a handwritten note from El. The note includes the affirmation line from the collector's own session. This is the differentiating detail — lock it now because it affects production and correspondence.

**Medium and paper.** You cannot describe the edition without knowing what it is printed on. Research and decide before the experiment goes live.

**Your enquiry email address.** One address, checked daily. Do not use a form — the mailto: opens their email client and you receive a real email from a real person.

### Lock when the first enquiry arrives

These only matter when there is a real collector in front of you.

**Print dimensions.** You can tell the first enquirer "I will confirm dimensions shortly" while you make the final call. Do not delay more than 48 hours.

**Specific production partner.** You need one before you ship, not before you sell.

**Whether the original requires a studio showing.** Depends entirely on who is asking and where they are. Decide in the moment.

**Framing.** Cross this bridge when asked. Default answer: "unframed, shipped rolled in an archival tube."

**International shipping cost.** Ask their location first, then confirm.

### Leave permanently undecided

**Return policy.** Fine art prints produced to order, signed, are non-returnable. This is standard. You do not need to publish a policy — if the question arises, state it plainly in conversation.

**Whether to offer payment plans.** This is a decision for a specific collector in a specific conversation, not a policy. If someone asks, El can choose.

**Future editions.** The Venus edition is 22, closed after 22. Whether a Ganymede edition or Persephone edition ever exists is a separate decision made when those chambers have artwork ready.

## Certificate Template

This is the document that ships with every print.

Draft it now. The design can be simple — clean typography, El's name, the work's title, space for a handwritten affirmation line and signature. It does not need to be elaborate. It needs to feel considered.

**Fields to include:**

```
ANCIENT TEMENOS
Venus — [Artwork title if it has one, otherwise "Venus Chamber"]

Medium:      Archival giclée print on [paper name]
Edition:     [N] of 22
Dimensions:  [h × w] cm / [h × w] in
Year:        2025 [or whenever the original was made]

This print was created from an original graphite work by Ellis Liu.
The edition is closed at 22. No further prints will be made from this image.

Affirmation:
[handwritten by El — the line from the collector's own Venus session]

                    ______________________
                    Ellis Liu
                    [Date of sale]
```

The affirmation line is the only part that is never printed — always handwritten by El in the space provided. This is the physical trace of the collector's session. It is what makes the certificate singular.

Design format: A5 or A4, landscape or portrait — El decides. Print on good paper (not the same paper as the artwork, but something with weight — 120gsm minimum). Keep one copy for El's records.

## Draft Collector Response Email

This is the email El sends within 24 hours of receiving the first enquiry. Rewrite in El's own voice — the structure is here, the words are a starting point.

There are two versions: one for someone who specified the original, one for someone who specified the print. If they didn't specify, use the combined version below.

El

**Version C — they didn't specify (most likely)**

Subject: Re: Venus Chamber — Artwork Enquiry

Thank you for writing.

Two things exist from the Venus chamber.

The original is a graphite work on [medium], [dimensions]. It is singular — one exists, and it will not be reproduced. I can send photographs and full documentation if you'd like to consider it.

El

---

Three notes on the tone of these emails:

"Let me know which is calling you" — this is intentional. It is the language of the temple carried into the correspondence. It signals the collector is still in the same world they entered through the Grimoire, not a different commercial one.

El never explains the price or apologises for it. She states it and moves on. If the collector doesn't respond, the work was not for them at this moment.

The emails are short. A collector reading a six-paragraph email about why the print is valuable is a collector being sold to. These emails assume the collector already knows what they want — they just need the facts and a human to respond to.

## What Collector-Ready Actually Means

Before the inquiry line goes into the Grimoire, El should be able to answer yes to these four questions:

1. If someone emails tonight, can I respond tomorrow with everything they need to make a decision?
2. Do I know the price of both the original and the print without having to think about it?
3. Do I have a high-resolution photograph of the original that I can attach?
4. Do I know exactly what the print is — paper, process, size, what ships with it?

If the answer to all four is yes: the experiment is ready to ship.
If any answer is no: resolve it first. The Grimoire line can wait one more day. A collector who enquires and receives a slow or uncertain response is not coming back.
